## 1. Purpose

1.1 This policy sets out how Harbour Mutual Bank manages the risks that arise from its use of information and communication technology, so that its services keep running when that technology fails or is attacked and so that it recovers quickly when they do not.

1.2 From 17 January 2025 Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC is subject to Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 on digital operational resilience for the financial sector. This policy and the documents that implement it form its ICT risk management framework. Harbour Mutual Bank plc applies the same framework as a matter of group policy.

## 2. Scope

2.1 This policy applies to Harbour Mutual Bank plc and Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC, to their staff and contractors, and to every ICT system, ICT asset and information asset either entity owns, uses or relies on.

2.2 It covers ICT Services provided within the Bank and by ICT Third-Party Service Providers, including NimbusCloud for core banking hosting and PayCore Ltd for card processing.

2.3 Risks from models are governed by the Model Risk Management Policy (HMB-POL-MRM-004). The ICT systems on which models run are within the scope of this policy.

## 3. Definitions

3.1 Terms with capital letters have the meaning the Policy Governance Framework (HMB-FRM-GOV-000) gives them.

3.2 "ICT risk" means any reasonably identifiable circumstance in relation to the use of network and information systems which, if it materialises, may compromise the security of those systems or of the services that depend on them.

## 4. Governance and responsibilities

4.1 The Management Body defines, approves, oversees and is responsible for the implementation of all arrangements related to the ICT risk management framework, and bears the ultimate responsibility for managing the Bank's ICT risk.

4.2 The Management Body sets the roles and responsibilities for ICT-related functions and allocates the budget for the Bank's digital operational resilience.

4.3 Members of the Management Body receive briefings on ICT risk from the Chief Information Officer when the Board's agenda calls for them.

4.4 The Head of Third-Party Risk monitors the arrangements the Bank has concluded with ICT Third-Party Service Providers under the ICT Third-Party Risk Standard (HMB-STD-ICT-011).

4.5 The Head of ICT Risk, in the second line of defence and reporting to the Chief Risk Officer, is responsible for overseeing ICT risk and is independent of the teams that run the Bank's ICT systems. The Bank keeps its ICT risk management, control and internal audit functions separate under the three lines of defence model.

4.6 Internal Audit audits the ICT risk management framework in line with its audit plan, using auditors with knowledge of ICT risk.

4.7 The Chief Information Officer owns this policy and is accountable for the operation of the Bank's ICT systems within its requirements.

## 5. ICT risk management framework and strategy

5.1 The ICT risk management framework is part of the Bank's overall risk management framework. It consists of this policy, the ICT Third-Party Risk Standard (HMB-STD-ICT-011), the ICT Incident Management Procedure (HMB-PRO-ICT-021) and the strategies, protocols and tools those documents describe.

5.2 The ICT risk management framework is documented and reviewed at least once a year, after any Major ICT-related Incident, and following supervisory instructions or conclusions drawn from digital operational resilience testing or audit. It is improved continuously on the basis of lessons from its implementation and monitoring.

5.3 The Bank provides complete and up-to-date information on its ICT risk and its ICT risk management framework to its competent authority on request.

5.4 The digital operational resilience strategy sets out how the ICT risk management framework supports the Bank's business strategy, the Bank's information security objectives and the ICT reference architecture. The Management Body approves it.

## 6. Identification

6.1 The Bank identifies, classifies and documents all its ICT supported business functions, the information assets and ICT assets that support them and their dependencies, and reviews this classification at least once a year.

6.2 The Bank keeps inventories of its information assets and ICT assets and updates them whenever a major change to its infrastructure occurs.

6.3 The Bank carries out an ICT risk assessment on each major change to its network and information system infrastructure.

6.4 The Bank identifies and documents every process that depends on an ICT Third-Party Service Provider, and the interconnections with providers that support Critical or Important Functions.

6.5 The Bank identifies the sources of ICT risk on a continuous basis and reviews the risk scenarios that affect it at least once a year.

## 7. Protection and prevention

7.1 The Bank monitors and controls the security and functioning of its ICT systems and keeps the impact of ICT risk on them as low as it can through ICT security tools, policies and procedures.

7.2 The Bank keeps rules that protect the availability, authenticity, integrity and confidentiality of data, information assets and ICT assets.

7.3 Access to information assets and ICT assets is limited to what approved functions need, under documented controls.

7.4 Every change to the Bank's ICT systems is made under its ICT change management process, which records, tests, assesses, approves, implements and verifies each change.

7.5 The Bank keeps documented rules for patches and updates.

7.6 The Bank uses strong authentication for access to its systems, protects its cryptographic keys and encrypts data according to its classification.

7.7 The Bank designs its network so that it can be segmented to contain an attack on interconnected financial processes.

## 8. Detection and incident management

8.1 The Bank has mechanisms to detect anomalous activities promptly, including ICT-related Incidents, with alert thresholds that start the incident response process.

8.2 The Chief Information Officer maintains the ICT Incident Management Procedure (HMB-PRO-ICT-021), which sets out how ICT-related Incidents are detected, recorded, classified, escalated and reported to the competent authority.

8.3 The Chief Information Officer informs the Management Body of every Major ICT-related Incident, its impact and the response.

8.4 The Bank devotes enough staff and tools to monitor user activity, ICT anomalies and ICT-related Incidents, in particular cyber-attacks.

## 9. Business continuity, response and recovery

9.1 The Bank keeps an ICT business continuity policy, set out in this section, as an integral part of its overall business continuity policy. It aims to keep the Bank's Critical or Important Functions running, to respond to and resolve ICT-related Incidents in a way that limits damage, and to resume activities and recover quickly.

9.2 The Bank implements the ICT business continuity policy through documented ICT business continuity plans and ICT response and recovery plans for each Critical or Important Function, which Internal Audit reviews independently.

9.3 ICT business continuity plans and ICT response and recovery plans are tested at least once a year, and after any substantive change to the ICT systems that support a Critical or Important Function. The tests include scenarios of cyber-attacks and switchovers between the primary ICT infrastructure and the redundant capacity, backups and redundant facilities.

9.4 The Bank conducts a business impact analysis of its exposure to severe business disruption and designs its ICT assets and ICT Services in line with it.

9.5 The Bank keeps backup policies and procedures that set the scope and minimum frequency of backup according to the criticality of the information, and keeps restoration and recovery procedures.

9.6 The Bank has a crisis management function, led by the Chief Operating Officer, which sets out procedures for internal and external crisis communications when the plans are activated.

9.7 The Bank keeps readily accessible records of what was done before and during each disruption in which its ICT business continuity plans or its ICT response and recovery plans were activated.

9.8 The Bank regularly reviews its ICT business continuity policy and its ICT response and recovery plans, taking into account the results of the tests in clause 9.3 and the recommendations of audits and supervisory reviews.

## 10. Digital operational resilience testing

10.1 The Bank keeps a digital operational resilience testing programme, following a risk-based approach, as part of its ICT risk management framework.

10.2 Tests are carried out by independent parties, internal or external.

10.3 Penetration tests are run at least once a year on the Bank's internet-facing systems.

10.4 Every issue a test reveals is prioritised, classified and remedied under a documented procedure.

10.5 Where the competent authority identifies Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC as required to carry out threat-led penetration testing, the Bank carries it out at least every three years.

## 11. ICT third-party risk

11.1 The Bank manages ICT third-party risk as part of ICT risk. Its strategy on ICT third-party risk is set out in the ICT Third-Party Risk Standard (HMB-STD-ICT-011).

11.2 The Management Body approves and periodically reviews the policy on arrangements regarding the use of ICT Services provided by ICT Third-Party Service Providers.

11.3 The Bank remains fully responsible for meeting its obligations under financial services law when it uses ICT Services provided by an ICT Third-Party Service Provider.

## 12. Training and awareness

12.1 ICT security awareness and digital operational resilience training are compulsory for every employee and member of senior management, at least once a year.

12.2 Staff with privileged access to ICT systems complete additional training on secure administration at least once a year.

## 13. Communication

13.1 The Bank has crisis communication plans for disclosing Major ICT-related Incidents to customers, counterparties and the public, as appropriate.

13.2 The Head of Communications is responsible for the Bank's public and media function during ICT-related Incidents.

## 14. Learning and evolving

14.1 After every Major ICT-related Incident that disrupts its core activities, the Bank carries out a post-incident review of the causes and the improvements needed.

14.2 The Chief Information Officer reports at least once a year to the Management Body on lessons from testing and from incidents.

## 15. Monitoring and review of this policy

15.1 The Head of ICT Risk monitors how the Bank meets this policy and reports breaches under the Policy Governance Framework (HMB-FRM-GOV-000).

15.2 The Chief Information Officer reviews this policy at least once a year.
