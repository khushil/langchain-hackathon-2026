# Policy Governance Framework

## Document control

Reference: HMB-FRM-GOV-000
Document type: Framework
Version: 2.0
Status: In force
Owner: Group Company Secretary
Approved by: Board of Directors of Harbour Mutual Bank plc
Approval date: 10 December 2025
Effective date: 1 January 2026
Next review: December 2027
Supersedes: HMB-FRM-GOV-000 version 1.4 of 6 June 2023

## 1. Purpose

1.1 This framework sets out how Harbour Mutual Bank writes, approves, publishes, reviews and withdraws the documents that govern how it is run. It establishes the hierarchy of those documents, the meaning of the terms they share, who may approve each kind of document and how often each kind is reviewed.

1.2 The framework exists so that every member of staff can find the rule that applies to the work in front of them, can tell which document prevails when two documents touch the same matter, and can see who owns each rule and who approved it.

1.3 Every policy, standard and procedure of the Bank is written and maintained under this framework. A document that has not been approved under this framework has no force, whatever its title.

## 2. Entities and scope

2.1 In this framework and in every document written under it, "the Bank" means Harbour Mutual Bank plc and its subsidiary Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC, taken together, unless a clause names one of them.

2.2 Harbour Mutual Bank plc is a bank incorporated in the United Kingdom. It is authorised by the Prudential Regulation Authority and regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority and the Prudential Regulation Authority. It provides current accounts, savings, residential mortgages, personal loans and card payments to about 1.2 million customers.

2.3 Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC is a credit institution authorised in Ireland and wholly owned by Harbour Mutual Bank plc. It provides savings accounts and personal loans to customers resident in Ireland, and it relies on the systems and services of Harbour Mutual Bank plc and of the Bank's suppliers to do so.

2.4 A document written under this framework applies to both entities unless it states that it applies to one of them only. Where a law or regulation binds one entity only, the document says which entity a clause applies to.

2.5 The board of directors of Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC adopts each group policy that applies to it by its own resolution. Until it has done so, the version of the policy it last adopted continues to apply to it.

## 3. Hierarchy of documents

3.1 The Bank's governing documents form a hierarchy of four levels: frameworks, policies, standards and procedures. A document at a lower level implements the document above it and may not contradict it.

3.2 A framework sets out how a whole area of the Bank's governance works. This document is the framework for all other governing documents.

3.3 A policy states the Bank's commitments in one area of risk or activity: what the Bank will do, who is accountable and what the Board expects. A policy is short enough to be read by the Board and is written in terms that do not change with the Bank's systems or suppliers.

3.4 A standard sets the minimum requirements that implement a policy in one area. A standard names the policy and the sections of it that it implements. A standard may set requirements more detailed than its policy and may set stricter requirements, and it may not set requirements weaker than its policy.

3.5 A procedure sets out the steps a team follows to meet a policy or a standard. A procedure names the policy or standard it implements. A procedure may be changed by its owner as the Bank's systems change, as long as it continues to meet the documents above it.

3.6 Where two documents at different levels are inconsistent, the document at the higher level prevails and the owner of the lower document corrects it within one month of the inconsistency being found.

3.7 Where two documents at the same level are inconsistent, the stricter requirement applies until the owners of both documents have agreed a correction and the approver of the documents has approved it.

3.8 Every governing document has a reference made of the letters HMB, the level (FRM, POL, STD or PRO), the area and a number. The reference stays the same across versions; the version number changes with each approved change.

## 4. Defined terms

4.1 The terms in this section have the meanings given here in every governing document of the Bank. A document that uses a defined term in another sense must define it for that document and say that it departs from this framework.

4.2 "Management Body" means the Board of Directors of Harbour Mutual Bank plc and, for any matter concerning Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC alone, the board of directors of Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC.

4.3 "Board Risk Committee" means the committee of the Board of Directors of Harbour Mutual Bank plc that advises the Board on risk appetite, risk exposures and the risk management framework.

4.4 "Executive Risk Committee" means the committee chaired by the Chief Risk Officer, with the Chief Executive Officer, the Chief Financial Officer, the Chief Information Officer, the Chief Operating Officer and the General Counsel as members, to which the Board delegates the approval of standards.

4.5 "Critical or Important Function" means a function of the Bank whose disruption would materially impair the Bank's financial performance, the soundness or continuity of its services and activities, or its ability to meet the conditions of its authorisation or its other obligations under financial services law.

4.6 "ICT Service" means a digital or data service provided through information and communication technology systems to one or more internal or external users on an ongoing basis, including hardware as a service, software, hosting and technical support.

4.7 "ICT Third-Party Service Provider" means an undertaking that provides ICT Services to the Bank, whether or not it belongs to the Bank's group.

4.8 "ICT-related Incident" means a single event or a series of linked events unplanned by the Bank that compromises the security of the network and information systems and has an adverse impact on the availability, authenticity, integrity or confidentiality of data, or on the services the Bank provides.

4.9 "Major ICT-related Incident" means an ICT-related Incident that has a high adverse impact on the network and information systems that support Critical or Important Functions, as classified under the ICT Incident Management Procedure (HMB-PRO-ICT-021).

4.10 "Model" means "a quantitative method, system, or approach that applies statistical, economic, financial, or mathematical theories, techniques, and assumptions to process input data into output" (SS1/23, 1.1(a)), as the Model Risk Management Policy (HMB-POL-MRM-004) sets out in full.

4.11 "AI System" means a machine-based system that is designed to operate with varying levels of autonomy, that may exhibit adaptiveness after deployment, and that infers from the input it receives how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations or decisions.

4.12 "Senior Manager" means a person approved by the Prudential Regulation Authority or the Financial Conduct Authority to perform a senior management function at Harbour Mutual Bank plc, and "SMF" followed by a number means the senior management function of that number.

4.13 "Document Owner" means the role named as owner in a governing document's document control block. The Document Owner keeps the document accurate, proposes changes and answers for the document's use.

## 5. Approval and delegation

5.1 The Board of Directors approves this framework and every policy. A policy is put to the Board with the recommendation of the Board committee whose remit covers it, which for every policy on risk is the Board Risk Committee.

5.2 The Board delegates the approval of standards to the Executive Risk Committee. The Executive Risk Committee may approve a standard only where the standard names the policy it implements and the Document Owner of that policy has confirmed in writing that the standard is consistent with it.

5.3 The approval of procedures is delegated to the executive who leads the function that follows the procedure, at the level of Chief Officer or a direct report of a Chief Officer. The approver records the approval in the document register.

5.4 A body or person to whom approval is delegated may not delegate it again. A delegation may be withdrawn by the Board at any time.

5.5 A change that corrects a spelling, a title, a role name or a cross-reference to another document, and changes no requirement, may be approved by the Document Owner alone. The Document Owner reports each such change to the next meeting of the approving body.

5.6 Before a document is put for approval, its Document Owner consults the second line of defence for the area concerned, the General Counsel and, where the document governs ICT, data or models, the Chief Information Security Officer, the Data Protection Officer or the Chief Risk Officer as the case may be. The approving body sees a record of the consultation.

## 6. Review cycle

6.1 Each policy and each standard is reviewed by its Document Owner at least once every two years from its effective date, and each procedure at least once a year.

6.2 A document is also reviewed when a law or regulation that it relies on changes, when an internal audit or a supervisory review finds that it is out of date, and when a change to a document above it in the hierarchy makes it inconsistent.

6.3 A review ends either with a new version approved under section 5, or with a record, approved by the body that approved the document, that the document remains fit for purpose without change.

6.4 The review date of a document is shown in its document control block under "Next review". A document whose review date has passed without a review remains in force, and the Group Company Secretary reports it to the Executive Risk Committee as overdue.

## 7. Exceptions and dispensations

7.1 An exception is a case in which a team cannot meet a requirement of a governing document for a time. A dispensation is the approval of an exception.

7.2 A team that cannot meet a requirement asks for a dispensation before the requirement is missed, giving the requirement, the reason, the risk the exception creates, the controls that will limit it and the date by which the requirement will be met.

7.3 A dispensation from a policy is approved by the Document Owner of the policy and the Chief Risk Officer together. A dispensation from a standard or a procedure is approved by its Document Owner, with the second line of defence consulted.

7.4 A dispensation lasts no longer than twelve months. It may be renewed once, for no longer than a further twelve months, by the body that approved the document.

7.5 The Group Company Secretary keeps a register of dispensations and reports the open dispensations to the Executive Risk Committee each quarter and to the Board Risk Committee each half year.

## 8. Breaches and escalation

8.1 A breach is a failure to meet a requirement of a governing document without a dispensation. Every member of staff who becomes aware of a breach reports it to their line manager and to the Document Owner.

8.2 The Document Owner records the breach, its cause and the action taken, and reports material breaches to the second line of defence within five business days.

8.3 The Chief Risk Officer reports material breaches of policies to the Board Risk Committee at its next meeting. A breach that may require notification to a regulator is reported to the General Counsel on the day it is found.

## 9. Roles

9.1 The Group Company Secretary owns this framework, keeps the document register, publishes approved documents to staff and reports on reviews, dispensations and breaches as this framework requires.

9.2 Each Document Owner writes and maintains their document, consults under clause 5.6, puts it for approval, leads its reviews and answers questions on its meaning.

9.3 The second line of defence, led by the Chief Risk Officer, challenges each document in its area before approval and reviews whether the documents of the Bank, taken together, cover the risks the Bank runs.

9.4 Internal Audit, led by the Chief Internal Auditor, reviews in its audit plan whether the governing documents are followed and whether this framework works as written.

## 10. Records and publication

10.1 The Group Company Secretary keeps a register of every governing document of the Bank, showing its reference, title, level, version, Document Owner, approver, approval date, effective date, next review date and the document it implements.

10.2 Each approved document is published to staff on the Bank's policy library on its effective date. A superseded version is marked as superseded, kept in the library beside its successor and kept for at least seven years after it was superseded.

10.3 The governing documents of the Bank in force on the effective date of this framework include the ICT Risk Management Policy (HMB-POL-ICT-001), the ICT Third-Party Risk Standard (HMB-STD-ICT-011), the ICT Incident Management Procedure (HMB-PRO-ICT-021) and the Model Risk Management Policy (HMB-POL-MRM-004). A document approved after that date enters the register on its approval.
