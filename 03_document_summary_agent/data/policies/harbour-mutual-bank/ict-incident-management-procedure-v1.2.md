# ICT Incident Management Procedure

Document type: Procedure
Version: 1.2
Status: In force
Owner: Head of ICT Operations
Approved by: Chief Information Officer, under the delegation in section 5 of the Policy Governance Framework (HMB-FRM-GOV-000)
Approval date: 2 September 2025
Effective date: 15 September 2025
Next review: September 2026

## 1. Purpose

1.1 This procedure implements section 8 of the ICT Risk Management Policy (HMB-POL-ICT-001). It sets out the steps the Bank follows to detect, record, classify, escalate, report, resolve and learn from ICT-related Incidents and significant cyber threats.

1.2 For Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC, this procedure is the ICT-related incident management process that Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 requires, and it sets out how major ICT-related incidents are reported to the competent authority.

1.3 The procedure is written for the ICT Operations teams who run the Bank's service desk and operations centre, for the Incident Managers on call, and for the second line of defence and the executives who are told of incidents.

## 2. Scope

2.1 This procedure applies to every ICT-related Incident and every significant cyber threat affecting Harbour Mutual Bank plc or Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC, including those that start at an ICT Third-Party Service Provider.

2.2 Incidents that affect only the physical security of buildings, or only the health and safety of staff, are handled under the Bank's business continuity arrangements and are outside this procedure unless they also affect ICT systems.

2.3 Personal data breaches are handled under this procedure and also under the Bank's data protection breach process, which the Data Protection Officer runs.

## 3. Definitions

3.1 Terms with capital letters have the meaning the Policy Governance Framework (HMB-FRM-GOV-000) gives them.

3.2 "Incident Manager" means the member of ICT Operations on call who takes charge of an incident from its classification until it is closed or handed to another Incident Manager.

3.3 "Significant Cyber Threat" means a cyber threat whose technical characteristics indicate that it could result in a Major ICT-related Incident or a major operational or security payment-related incident.

3.4 "Priority" means the order in which incidents are worked on, from P1, the highest, to P4, the lowest.

## 4. Detection and recording

4.1 Every ICT-related Incident and every Significant Cyber Threat is recorded in the Bank's incident management system, whoever detects it and however it is detected. The record holds the time of detection, the services affected, the actions taken and their times, and the people involved.

4.2 The operations centre watches the early warning indicators set for each service, including error rates, response times, failed log-in attempts, unusual data transfers and alerts from the Bank's security monitoring tools, and opens an incident record whenever an indicator crosses its threshold.

4.3 A customer complaint that suggests an ICT system has failed or been compromised is logged as a possible incident by Customer Operations and passed to the operations centre at once.

4.4 An ICT Third-Party Service Provider that reports an incident affecting the Bank's services is logged in the same way, and the Business Owner of the Arrangement is told.

## 5. Classification

5.1 The Incident Manager classifies each ICT-related Incident by its priority and severity and by the criticality of the services affected, and determines its impact using these criteria: (a) the number and relevance of clients or financial counterparts affected and, where applicable, the amount or number of transactions affected, and whether the incident has caused reputational impact; (b) the duration of the incident, including the service downtime; (c) the geographical spread of the areas affected, in particular whether more than two Member States are affected; (d) the data losses the incident entails, in relation to availability, authenticity, integrity or confidentiality; (e) the criticality of the services affected, including the Bank's transactions and operations; and (f) the economic impact, in particular the direct and indirect costs and losses, in absolute and relative terms.

5.2 An incident is classified as a Major ICT-related Incident when it meets the materiality thresholds set in the regulatory technical standards adopted under Regulation (EU) 2022/2554. The Head of ICT Risk keeps those thresholds in the classification guide in the incident management system.

5.3 A cyber threat is classified as a Significant Cyber Threat on the basis of the criticality of the services at risk, the number and relevance of the clients or financial counterparts targeted and the geographical spread of the areas at risk.

5.4 The Incident Manager reviews the classification whenever new information about the incident becomes available, and records each change of classification with its reason.

## 6. Roles

6.1 The Incident Manager leads the response to an incident, decides which teams take part, keeps the incident record and makes the escalations in section 7.

6.2 For each incident type in the classification guide, the guide names the roles that are activated, including the technical teams, the Business Owner of any Arrangement concerned, the Data Protection Officer for incidents involving personal data and the Head of Communications for incidents visible to customers.

6.3 The Head of ICT Operations owns this procedure, makes sure an Incident Manager is on call at all hours and decides when a P1 incident is closed.

6.4 The Head of ICT Risk, in the second line of defence, checks the classification of every incident of priority P1 or P2, decides whether an incident is to be reported to the competent authority and makes those reports under section 8.

## 7. Escalation

7.1 An incident of priority P1 is escalated to the Head of ICT Operations within thirty minutes of its classification, and an incident of priority P2 within two hours.

7.2 Every Major ICT-related Incident is reported to the Chief Information Officer and to the Chief Risk Officer within two hours of its classification, with its impact and the response so far.

7.3 The Management Body is informed of Major ICT-related Incidents through the quarterly ICT risk report that the Head of ICT Risk presents to the Board Risk Committee, which explains for each incident its impact, the response and the additional controls put in place as a result.

7.4 An incident that involves an ICT Third-Party Service Provider is also escalated to the Head of Third-Party Risk, who invokes the provider's incident duties under the ICT Third-Party Risk Standard (HMB-STD-ICT-011).

7.5 Customer complaints linked to an open incident are passed to the Incident Manager and are answered under the Bank's complaints process once the incident has been resolved.

## 8. Reporting to the competent authority

8.1 For each Major ICT-related Incident affecting Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC, the Head of ICT Risk submits to its competent authority an initial notification; an intermediate report as soon as the status of the incident has changed significantly or its handling has changed on the basis of new information, followed by updated notifications each time a relevant status update is available and whenever the competent authority asks for one; and a final report when the root cause analysis has been completed and the actual impact figures are available to replace estimates.

8.2 Each notification and report is made within the time limits, and on the templates, set in the technical standards adopted under Article 20 of Regulation (EU) 2022/2554. The Head of ICT Risk keeps those time limits in the reporting calendar of the incident management system and checks them against each incident record.

8.3 If a technical problem prevents the use of the template for the initial notification, the Head of ICT Risk notifies the competent authority by the alternative means it has published and records that it did so.

8.4 The Head of ICT Risk may notify a Significant Cyber Threat to the competent authority on a voluntary basis where the threat is of relevance to the financial system, to service users or to clients.

8.5 The Bank does not outsource its reporting to the competent authority. Where an ICT Third-Party Service Provider supplies information for a report, the Head of ICT Risk checks it before the report is made.

8.6 Harbour Mutual Bank plc reports incidents to its own regulators under the Bank's regulatory notification process, which the General Counsel owns.

## 9. Communication

9.1 While a Major ICT-related Incident is open, the Head of Communications prepares and issues the Bank's statements to the media and keeps staff informed through the incident bulletin, agreeing each statement with the Incident Manager and the Chief Risk Officer.

9.2 Financial entities that act as the Bank's counterparties are informed of an incident that affects them, as appropriate, by the Business Owner of the service concerned.

9.3 Communications to staff distinguish between those who take part in the response and those who need to be told of it.

## 10. Response and recovery

10.1 The Incident Manager follows the response steps in the classification guide for the incident type, to contain the incident, limit its impact and bring the services affected back into operation in a secure state as quickly as possible.

10.2 Where the response requires the ICT business continuity plans or the ICT response and recovery plans to be activated, the Incident Manager asks the crisis management function to activate them and records the time of activation.

10.3 Before a service is restored after an incident that affected data, the technical teams check the integrity of the data, including reconciliations with data received from external parties, and the Incident Manager records the checks.

## 11. Closure and post-incident review

11.1 An incident is closed only when its root causes have been identified, documented and addressed, or when the Head of ICT Operations has accepted a plan to address them with dates and owners.

11.2 After each Major ICT-related Incident that disrupted the Bank's core activities, the Head of ICT Operations leads a post-incident review within twenty business days of closure. The review determines whether this procedure was followed and whether the actions taken were effective, including the promptness of the response to security alerts, the quality and speed of any forensic analysis, the effectiveness of escalation within the Bank and the effectiveness of internal and external communication.

11.3 The review's findings and the improvements agreed are recorded in the incident management system and fed into the ICT risk assessment. The Head of ICT Risk communicates the changes made to the competent authority when it asks for them.

## 12. Records and exercises

12.1 Incident records, notifications, reports and review findings are kept for at least seven years from the closure of the incident.

12.2 The Head of ICT Operations exercises this procedure at least twice a year, including at least one exercise of the reporting steps in section 8, and records the lessons learned.

12.3 The Head of ICT Operations reviews this procedure at least once a year under section 6 of the Policy Governance Framework (HMB-FRM-GOV-000).
