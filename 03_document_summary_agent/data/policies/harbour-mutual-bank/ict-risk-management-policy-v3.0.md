## 1. Purpose

1.1 This policy sets out how Harbour Mutual Bank identifies, protects against, detects, responds to, recovers from and learns from risks that arise from its use of information and communication technology, and how it keeps its services running when that technology fails or is attacked.

1.2 For Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC, this policy and the documents that implement it form the ICT risk management framework that Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 on digital operational resilience for the financial sector requires of a credit institution. For Harbour Mutual Bank plc, this policy applies the same framework as a matter of group policy.

1.3 This version restructures the policy so that the Board approves the Bank's commitments and the detailed requirements sit in the standards and procedures that implement it. It moves the Bank's ICT business continuity requirements out of this policy into a standard of their own and aligns the review of this policy with the review cycle of the Policy Governance Framework (HMB-FRM-GOV-000).

## 2. Scope

2.1 This policy applies to Harbour Mutual Bank plc and to Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC, to every member of their staff, to contractors working on their behalf and to every ICT system, ICT asset and information asset that either entity owns, uses or relies on, wherever it is hosted.

2.2 It covers ICT Services provided within the Bank and ICT Services provided by ICT Third-Party Service Providers, including the services NimbusCloud provides for core banking hosting and the services PayCore Ltd provides for card processing.

2.3 Risks from models and from AI Systems are governed by the Model Risk Management Policy (HMB-POL-MRM-004) and the Artificial Intelligence Use Standard (HMB-STD-AI-014). The ICT systems that run those models and AI Systems are within the scope of this policy.

## 3. Definitions

3.1 Terms with capital letters have the meaning the Policy Governance Framework (HMB-FRM-GOV-000) gives them in its section 4.

3.2 "ICT risk" means any reasonably identifiable circumstance in relation to the use of network and information systems which, if it materialises, may compromise the security of those systems, of any technology-dependent tool or process, of operations and processes, or of the provision of services, by producing adverse effects in the digital or physical environment.

3.3 "Digital operational resilience" means the ability of the Bank to build, assure and review its operational integrity and reliability by ensuring, directly or through ICT Third-Party Service Providers, the full range of ICT-related capabilities needed to address the security of the network and information systems it uses, and which support the continued provision of its services and their quality, including through disruptions.

## 4. Governance and responsibilities

4.1 The Management Body defines, approves, oversees and is responsible for the implementation of all arrangements related to the ICT risk management framework of the Bank, and bears the ultimate responsibility for managing the Bank's ICT risk.

4.2 The Management Body sets clear roles and responsibilities for all ICT-related functions, puts in place the governance arrangements under which those functions communicate, cooperate and coordinate in a timely way, and allocates and periodically reviews the budget that the Bank's digital operational resilience needs.

4.3 The Management Body sets and approves the digital operational resilience strategy described in section 5 and approves, oversees and periodically reviews the Bank's ICT business continuity arrangements described in section 9.

4.4 Each member of the Management Body completes training on ICT risk and its effect on the Bank's operations at least once a year, at a depth commensurate with the ICT risk the Bank manages, so that they can understand and assess that risk. The Group Company Secretary keeps the record of the training completed.

4.5 The Head of Third-Party Risk holds the role of monitoring the arrangements the Bank has concluded with ICT Third-Party Service Providers on the use of ICT Services, and oversees the related risk exposure and its documentation under the ICT Third-Party Risk Standard (HMB-STD-ICT-011).

4.6 The responsibility for managing and overseeing ICT risk is assigned to the Head of ICT Risk, who leads a control function in the second line of defence, reports to the Chief Risk Officer and is independent of the Chief Information Officer's teams that run the Bank's ICT systems. The Bank keeps its ICT risk management functions, its control functions and its internal audit function separate and independent of each other under the three lines of defence model.

4.7 Internal Audit audits the ICT risk management framework at least once a year in line with the audit plan approved by the Audit Committee. The auditors assigned have knowledge, skills and expertise in ICT risk and are independent of the functions they audit. Critical ICT audit findings are tracked to closure by the Head of ICT Risk, who reports their status to the Board Risk Committee each quarter.

4.8 The Management Body approves and periodically reviews the Bank's ICT internal audit plans, the ICT audits and any material change to them, on the recommendation of the Audit Committee.

4.9 The Chief Information Security Officer owns this policy, maintains the standards and procedures that implement it in the areas of information security, detection and response, and reports to the Executive Risk Committee each quarter on how the Bank meets this policy.

4.10 The Chief Information Officer is accountable for the operation of the Bank's ICT systems within the requirements of this policy and for the ICT Incident Management Procedure (HMB-PRO-ICT-021).

## 5. ICT risk management framework and strategy

5.1 The Bank's ICT risk management framework is part of its overall risk management framework. It consists of this policy, the ICT Third-Party Risk Standard (HMB-STD-ICT-011), the ICT Incident Management Procedure (HMB-PRO-ICT-021), the ICT Business Continuity Standard (HMB-STD-ICT-019) and the strategies, protocols and tools those documents describe, and it is documented in them.

5.2 The ICT risk management framework is reviewed in line with the review cycle set for policies in section 6 of the Policy Governance Framework (HMB-FRM-GOV-000), and also after any Major ICT-related Incident. The Head of ICT Risk records each review and the improvements it leads to.

5.3 The Bank provides complete and up-to-date information on its ICT risk and on its ICT risk management framework to its competent authority on request, and submits a report on the latest review of the framework when the competent authority asks for one.

5.4 The digital operational resilience strategy sets out how the ICT risk management framework supports the Bank's business strategy and objectives, the Bank's information security objectives with their key performance indicators and key risk metrics, the ICT reference architecture and any changes to it that the business strategy needs, the mechanisms the Bank uses to detect ICT-related Incidents and to protect against them, and the testing programme described in section 10.

5.5 The Chief Information Security Officer proposes the digital operational resilience strategy, the Board Risk Committee reviews it and the Management Body approves it. The Chief Information Security Officer reports progress against the strategy to the Board Risk Committee twice a year.

## 6. Identification

6.1 The Bank identifies, classifies and documents all its ICT supported business functions, the roles and responsibilities for them, the information assets and ICT assets that support those functions, and their roles and dependencies in relation to ICT risk. The Head of ICT Risk reviews the adequacy of this classification and of its documentation as needed and at least once a year.

6.2 The Bank keeps inventories of its information assets and ICT assets, including those on remote sites, network resources and hardware, maps those it considers critical and maps their configuration and the links and dependencies between them. The inventories are updated at least once a year and whenever a major change to the network and information system infrastructure occurs.

6.3 The Bank carries out an ICT risk assessment on each major change in the network and information system infrastructure and in the processes or procedures that affect its ICT supported business functions, information assets or ICT assets.

6.4 The Bank identifies and documents every process that depends on an ICT Third-Party Service Provider, and identifies the interconnections with ICT Third-Party Service Providers that provide services supporting Critical or Important Functions.

6.5 The Bank identifies on a continuous basis all sources of ICT risk, including its exposure to and from other financial entities, and assesses the cyber threats and ICT vulnerabilities relevant to its ICT supported business functions and assets. The risk scenarios that affect the Bank are reviewed at least once a year.

## 7. Protection and prevention

7.1 The Bank continuously monitors and controls the security and functioning of its ICT systems and tools, and keeps the impact of ICT risk on them as low as it can through ICT security tools, policies and procedures.

7.2 The Chief Information Security Officer maintains the Bank's information security rules, which protect the availability, authenticity, integrity and confidentiality of data, information assets and ICT assets, including those of customers. The rules cover the security of data in transfer, at rest and in use.

7.3 Access to information assets and ICT assets is limited to what is needed for approved functions and activities. Access rights are granted, reviewed and removed under documented controls, and privileged access is reviewed each quarter.

7.4 Every change to software, hardware, firmware, systems or security parameters is made under the Bank's ICT change management process. The process is based on an assessment of risk, is part of the Bank's overall change management process and makes sure that every change is recorded, tested, assessed, approved, implemented and verified in a controlled way. The process is approved by the Chief Information Officer and the Chief Information Security Officer.

7.5 The Bank keeps documented rules for patches and updates, under which security patches rated critical are applied to internet-facing systems within fourteen days of release and to other systems within thirty days, unless a dispensation is granted under the Policy Governance Framework (HMB-FRM-GOV-000).

7.6 The Bank uses strong authentication based on recognised standards for access to its systems, protects cryptographic keys, and encrypts data according to its classification and to the results of the ICT risk assessment.

7.7 The Bank designs its network so that it can be severed or segmented at once to contain an attack, in particular for interconnected financial processes.

## 8. Detection and incident management

8.1 The Bank has mechanisms to detect anomalous activities promptly, including network performance issues and ICT-related Incidents, and to identify potential material single points of failure. The detection mechanisms set alert thresholds and criteria that start the incident response process and alert the staff in charge of incident response automatically.

8.2 The Chief Information Officer maintains the ICT Incident Management Procedure (HMB-PRO-ICT-021), which sets out how ICT-related Incidents and significant cyber threats are detected, recorded, classified, escalated, reported to the competent authority and closed.

8.3 The Head of ICT Operations informs the Chief Information Officer and the Chief Risk Officer of every Major ICT-related Incident without delay. The Chief Risk Officer informs the Management Body of every Major ICT-related Incident without delay, explaining its impact, the response and any additional controls to be put in place as a result.

8.4 The Bank devotes enough staff and tools to monitor user activity, the occurrence of ICT anomalies and ICT-related Incidents, in particular cyber-attacks, at all hours.

## 9. Business continuity, response and recovery

9.1 The ICT business continuity policy of the Bank is set out in the ICT Business Continuity Standard (HMB-STD-ICT-019), which forms part of the Bank's overall business continuity arrangements and which the Chief Operating Officer owns.

9.2 The Bank conducts a business impact analysis of its exposure to severe business disruption at least once a year, and designs and uses its ICT assets and ICT Services in line with it, in particular so that every critical component has the redundancy the analysis requires.

9.3 The Bank keeps backup policies and procedures that set the scope of the data subject to backup and the minimum frequency of backup according to the criticality of the information and the confidentiality of the data, and keeps restoration and recovery procedures and methods. Backups are restored to systems that are physically and logically separate from the source systems.

9.4 The Bank has a crisis management function, led by the Chief Operating Officer, which takes charge when the ICT business continuity plans or the ICT response and recovery plans are activated and sets out the procedures for internal and external crisis communications.

9.5 The Bank keeps readily accessible records of what was done before and during each disruption in which its ICT business continuity plans or its ICT response and recovery plans were activated.

## 10. Digital operational resilience testing

10.1 The Bank keeps a digital operational resilience testing programme as part of its ICT risk management framework, to assess its readiness to handle ICT-related Incidents, to find weaknesses, deficiencies and gaps in its digital operational resilience and to put corrective measures in place promptly. The programme follows a risk-based approach and takes account of the criticality of the Bank's information assets and services.

10.2 Tests are carried out by independent parties, internal or external. Where an internal team carries out a test, the Head of ICT Risk confirms that it has enough resources and no conflict of interest in the design or execution of the test.

10.3 Penetration tests are run at least once a year on the Bank's internet-facing systems.

10.4 Every issue that a test reveals is prioritised, classified and remedied under a documented procedure, and the Head of ICT Risk validates that each weakness, deficiency or gap has been fully addressed before the issue is closed.

10.5 Where the competent authority identifies Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC as required to carry out threat-led penetration testing, the Bank carries it out at least every three years, covering the Critical or Important Functions the competent authority validates, with the participation of the ICT Third-Party Service Providers concerned.

## 11. ICT third-party risk

11.1 The Bank manages ICT third-party risk as part of ICT risk within this framework. Its strategy on ICT third-party risk, including its policy on the use of ICT Services supporting Critical or Important Functions provided by ICT Third-Party Service Providers, is set out in the ICT Third-Party Risk Standard (HMB-STD-ICT-011), which implements this section.

11.2 The Management Body approves and periodically reviews the policy on arrangements regarding the use of ICT Services provided by ICT Third-Party Service Providers, and regularly reviews the risks identified in the contractual arrangements on the use of ICT Services supporting Critical or Important Functions.

11.3 The Management Body is informed, through the Board Risk Committee, of the arrangements concluded with ICT Third-Party Service Providers, of any relevant planned material change regarding those providers and of the potential impact of such a change on the Critical or Important Functions the arrangements support.

11.4 The Bank remains fully responsible at all times for meeting all its obligations under financial services law when it uses ICT Services provided by an ICT Third-Party Service Provider.

## 12. Training and awareness

12.1 ICT security awareness and digital operational resilience training are compulsory modules of the Bank's staff training. Every employee and every member of senior management completes them when they join and at least once a year after that, at a level of complexity that matches the remit of their role.

12.2 Staff with privileged access to ICT systems complete additional training on secure administration at least once a year.

12.3 The Bank includes ICT Third-Party Service Providers in its awareness training where the ICT Third-Party Risk Standard (HMB-STD-ICT-011) provides for it.

## 13. Communication

13.1 The Bank has crisis communication plans that allow it to disclose Major ICT-related Incidents and major vulnerabilities to customers, counterparties and the public in a responsible way, as appropriate.

13.2 The Head of Communications is responsible for carrying out the Bank's communication strategy for ICT-related Incidents and for its public and media function during them.

13.3 Communications to staff distinguish between the staff who take part in ICT risk management, in particular those responsible for response and recovery, and the staff who need to be informed.

## 14. Learning and evolving

14.1 After every Major ICT-related Incident that disrupts the Bank's core activities, the Bank carries out a post-incident review that analyses the causes of the disruption and identifies the improvements needed to its ICT operations or to its ICT business continuity arrangements.

14.2 Lessons from testing under section 10, from real incidents and from the activation of the ICT business continuity plans are fed into the ICT risk assessment on a continuous basis and form the basis for reviews of the relevant parts of this framework.

14.3 The Chief Information Security Officer reports at least once a year to the Management Body on those lessons and on the evolution of the Bank's ICT risk, and makes recommendations.

14.4 The Bank monitors relevant technological developments on a continuous basis, to understand how deploying new technologies may affect its ICT security requirements and its digital operational resilience.

## 15. Monitoring and review of this policy

15.1 The Head of ICT Risk monitors how the Bank meets this policy and reports breaches under section 8 of the Policy Governance Framework (HMB-FRM-GOV-000).

15.2 Dispensations from this policy are granted only under section 7 of the Policy Governance Framework (HMB-FRM-GOV-000).

15.3 The Chief Information Security Officer reviews this policy under section 6 of the Policy Governance Framework (HMB-FRM-GOV-000).
