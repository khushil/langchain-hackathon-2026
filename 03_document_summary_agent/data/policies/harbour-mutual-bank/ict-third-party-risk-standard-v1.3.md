# ICT Third-Party Risk Standard

## Document control

Reference: HMB-STD-ICT-011
Document type: Standard
Version: 1.3
Status: In force
Owner: Head of Third-Party Risk
Approved by: Executive Risk Committee, under the delegation in section 5 of the Policy Governance Framework (HMB-FRM-GOV-000)
Approval date: 20 May 2026
Effective date: 1 June 2026
Next review: May 2028

## 1. Purpose

1.1 This standard implements section 11 of the ICT Risk Management Policy (HMB-POL-ICT-001). It sets the minimum requirements for choosing, contracting, monitoring and leaving ICT Third-Party Service Providers, so that the Bank manages ICT third-party risk as part of its ICT risk.

1.2 For Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC, this standard sets out the Bank's strategy on ICT third-party risk and its arrangements for the sound management of that risk under Regulation (EU) 2022/2554.

1.3 This version adds the requirements for arrangements with providers of public cloud services, records the Bank's current arrangements supporting Critical or Important Functions and aligns the register of information with the templates the Bank uses for its competent authority.

## 2. Scope

2.1 This standard applies to every contractual arrangement under which Harbour Mutual Bank plc or Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC uses an ICT Service provided by an ICT Third-Party Service Provider, whether the provider is outside the Bank's group or within it.

2.2 It applies from the moment a business unit first considers using an ICT Service from a provider until the arrangement has ended and the Bank's data has been returned or destroyed.

2.3 Arrangements that are not ICT Services, such as the purchase of office supplies, are outside this standard and are governed by the Bank's procurement rules.

## 3. Definitions

3.1 Terms with capital letters have the meaning the Policy Governance Framework (HMB-FRM-GOV-000) gives them.

3.2 "Arrangement" means a contractual arrangement on the use of ICT Services between the Bank and an ICT Third-Party Service Provider, with its schedules, service level agreements and any later amendments.

3.3 "Business Owner" means the executive accountable for the business service that an Arrangement supports.

3.4 "Public Cloud Service" means an ICT Service, provided from shared infrastructure that the provider operates for many customers, through which the Bank uses computing, storage or software.

## 4. Strategy and policy on ICT third-party risk

4.1 The Bank's strategy on ICT third-party risk is set out in this standard. It includes the Bank's policy on the use of ICT Services supporting Critical or Important Functions provided by ICT Third-Party Service Providers, and it applies to each of Harbour Mutual Bank plc and Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC on an individual basis and to the Bank on a consolidated basis.

4.2 Under that policy the Bank uses an ICT Third-Party Service Provider to support a Critical or Important Function only where the Arrangement meets sections 6, 8 and 10 of this standard, the Business Owner has accepted the residual risk and the Executive Risk Committee has approved the Arrangement.

4.3 The Executive Risk Committee reviews this strategy at least once a year. The Management Body, through the Board Risk Committee, reviews each year the risks identified in respect of the Arrangements supporting Critical or Important Functions, on the basis of an assessment of the Bank's overall risk profile.

4.4 In setting the strategy the Bank considers its dependence on single providers across the group, and records the reasons for its choice of providers for each Critical or Important Function.

## 5. Register of information

5.1 The Head of Third-Party Risk maintains and updates a register of information on every Arrangement, at the level of each entity, at sub-consolidated level where one exists and at consolidated level. The register distinguishes the Arrangements that cover ICT Services supporting Critical or Important Functions from those that do not.

5.2 Each year the Head of Third-Party Risk reports to the competent authority of Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC the number of new Arrangements, the categories of ICT Third-Party Service Providers, the types of Arrangement and the ICT Services and functions being provided.

5.3 The Bank makes the full register, or the sections the competent authority asks for, available to the competent authority on request.

5.4 The Head of Third-Party Risk informs the competent authority in good time of any planned Arrangement for ICT Services supporting a Critical or Important Function, and of any function that has become critical or important.

5.5 Business Owners confirm the accuracy of the register entries for their Arrangements each quarter.

## 6. Assessment before an Arrangement is entered into

6.1 Before entering into an Arrangement, the Business Owner, with the Head of Third-Party Risk, assesses whether the Arrangement covers ICT Services supporting a Critical or Important Function, assesses whether the supervisory conditions for contracting are met, identifies and assesses all relevant risks of the Arrangement, including whether it may add to ICT concentration risk, undertakes due diligence on the prospective provider and identifies and assesses any conflict of interest the Arrangement may cause.

6.2 The Bank enters into Arrangements only with providers that meet appropriate information security standards. For an Arrangement supporting a Critical or Important Function, the Chief Information Security Officer confirms before it is concluded that the provider uses the most up-to-date and highest quality information security standards.

6.3 The due diligence covers the provider's financial standing, its ownership, its ICT security, its business continuity arrangements, its subcontractors, the locations from which it provides services and the law that would apply to the Bank's data if the provider failed.

6.4 The results of the assessment are recorded in the register before the Arrangement is signed.

## 7. Concentration and subcontracting

7.1 When assessing an Arrangement supporting a Critical or Important Function, the Head of Third-Party Risk considers whether it would mean contracting a provider that is not easily substitutable, or holding several Arrangements supporting Critical or Important Functions with the same provider or with closely connected providers, and weighs the benefits and costs of alternative solutions.

7.2 Where an Arrangement allows the provider to subcontract an ICT Service supporting a Critical or Important Function, the Head of Third-Party Risk weighs the benefits and risks of the subcontracting, in particular where a subcontractor is established outside the United Kingdom and the European Union, and assesses whether long or complex chains of subcontracting would limit the Bank's ability to monitor the function.

7.3 For a provider established in a third country, the assessment also considers the protection of personal data and the effective enforcement of the law in that country.

## 8. Contractual provisions

8.1 The rights and obligations of the Bank and of the provider are set out in writing. The full contract, including its service level agreements, is kept as one written document in a durable format.

8.2 Every Arrangement includes at least the following: (a) a clear and complete description of all functions and ICT Services the provider is to provide, stating whether subcontracting of an ICT Service supporting a Critical or Important Function is permitted and, if so, on what conditions; (b) the regions or countries where the functions and ICT Services are to be provided and where data is to be processed and stored, and the provider's duty to notify the Bank in advance if it plans to change any of them; (c) provisions on the availability, authenticity, integrity and confidentiality of data, including personal data; (d) provisions on access to, recovery of and return of the Bank's data in an easily accessible format if the provider becomes insolvent, enters resolution or stops its business, or if the Arrangement ends; (e) service level descriptions, with their updates and revisions; (f) the provider's duty to cooperate fully with the competent authorities and resolution authorities of the Bank, including persons they appoint; and (g) termination rights and the minimum notice periods for termination.

8.3 Every Arrangement supporting a Critical or Important Function also includes: (a) full service level descriptions with precise quantitative and qualitative performance targets; (b) notice periods and reporting duties of the provider, including notice of any development that might materially affect its ability to provide the ICT Services; (c) the provider's duty to implement and test business contingency plans and to have ICT security measures, tools and policies that give an appropriate level of security; (d) the provider's duty to take part in, and cooperate fully with, the Bank's threat-led penetration testing; (e) unrestricted rights of access, inspection and audit for the Bank, for a third party it appoints and for the competent authority, with the right to take copies of relevant documents on site; and (f) an exit strategy with a mandatory transition period during which the provider continues to provide the services while the Bank moves to another provider or brings the services in-house.

8.4 For an Arrangement with a provider of Public Cloud Services, including the Bank's Arrangement with NimbusCloud, the Bank accepts pooled audits organised with the provider's other customers, and the provider's independent certifications and audit reports, in place of the rights of access, inspection and audit in clause 8.3, point (e).

8.5 When negotiating an Arrangement, the Bank considers using standard contractual clauses developed by public authorities for the service concerned.

8.6 The General Counsel approves every departure from clauses 8.1 to 8.3 before the Arrangement is signed and records it in the register.

## 9. Termination

9.1 Every Arrangement allows the Bank to terminate it where the provider significantly breaches applicable law, regulation or the contract; where monitoring finds circumstances that may alter the performance of the functions provided, including material changes affecting the Arrangement or the provider; where the provider shows weaknesses in its overall ICT risk management, in particular in how it keeps data available, authentic, intact and confidential; and where the competent authority can no longer effectively supervise the Bank because of the Arrangement.

9.2 The Business Owner, with the Head of Third-Party Risk and the General Counsel, decides whether to exercise a termination right and reports the decision to the Executive Risk Committee.

## 10. Exit strategies

10.1 For every Arrangement supporting a Critical or Important Function, the Head of Third-Party Risk keeps a documented exit plan that identifies alternative solutions and sets out a transition plan for moving the ICT Services and the Bank's data to another provider, or back into the Bank, securely and in full. Exit plans are reviewed every two years.

10.2 Each exit plan considers the failure of the provider, a deterioration in the quality of its services, a business disruption caused by inappropriate or failed provision of the services and the termination of the Arrangement under section 9, and shows how the Bank would leave without disrupting its business, without limiting its ability to meet regulatory requirements and without harm to the continuity and quality of services to customers.

10.3 The Business Owner keeps contingency measures that maintain the business service if the provider fails before the exit plan has been completed.

## 11. Monitoring of providers

11.1 The Business Owner monitors each provider's performance against its service levels each month for Arrangements supporting Critical or Important Functions and each quarter for the others, and reports breaches of service levels to the Head of Third-Party Risk.

11.2 The Head of Third-Party Risk carries out a full review of each provider supporting a Critical or Important Function at least once a year, covering its performance, its financial standing, its ICT security and any change in its subcontracting.

11.3 The Head of Third-Party Risk sets in advance, on a risk-based approach, the frequency of audits and inspections of each provider and the areas to be audited, using commonly accepted audit standards, and checks that the auditors have the skills to audit Arrangements of high technical complexity.

11.4 The Head of Third-Party Risk reports to the Executive Risk Committee each quarter on the Bank's Arrangements, their risks and the providers' performance.

## 12. Roles

12.1 The Head of Third-Party Risk owns this standard, maintains the register and the exit plans, and holds the role of monitoring the Bank's Arrangements under clause 4.5 of the ICT Risk Management Policy (HMB-POL-ICT-001).

12.2 Each Business Owner is accountable for the Arrangements that support their business service and for meeting this standard in them.

12.3 The General Counsel is responsible for the contractual terms of each Arrangement, and the Chief Information Security Officer for the assessment of each provider's information security.

12.4 The Head of ICT Risk challenges the assessments made under sections 6 and 7 and reviews whether this standard is followed.

## 13. Arrangements supporting Critical or Important Functions

13.1 On the effective date of this version, the Bank's Arrangements supporting Critical or Important Functions are with NimbusCloud, for core banking hosting, under a contract ending on 30 June 2027, and with PayCore Ltd, for card processing, under a contract ending on 31 December 2029.

13.2 The Arrangement of Harbour Mutual Bank Europe DAC with Brindlemoor Analytics Ltd for the consumer credit scoring service supports its consumer lending, which the Bank treats as an important function, and is managed under this standard and under the Artificial Intelligence Use Standard (HMB-STD-AI-014).

13.3 The Head of Third-Party Risk updates this section through the register when an Arrangement supporting a Critical or Important Function starts or ends, and the next version of this standard records the change.
